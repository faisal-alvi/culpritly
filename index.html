The problem

The worst breakages don't look broken

An update or a settings change lands. The site still loads and the product pages look fine. But there is a JavaScript error on the checkout or signup page, or the payment gateway has stopped responding, and every customer who tries to pay quietly gives up.

There is no white screen and no PHP error, so nothing raises a flag. WordPress's built-in rollback and uptime monitors only catch crashes. Most owners find out days later, from a customer or a sales report, and then pay a developer for hours of digging through logs to work out which change did it.

How it works

From silent failure to a clear answer

  1. 01

    Install the connector

    Add the lightweight Culpritly plugin to your WooCommerce store or membership site. It sends small batches of events to Culpritly and stays out of your customers' way.

  2. 02

    Culpritly records and watches

    Every change is logged: plugin, theme and core updates, activations, and key WooCommerce and payment settings. Culpritly also watches checkout health: failed payments, checkout and signup errors, payment scripts that fail to load, a site that slows down under load, and orders slowing or stopping compared with your normal pace.

    Monitoring runs on our servers, so you still get alerted if your site is down.

  3. 03

    You get one clear alert

    When something breaks, Culpritly's AI incident analyst writes you a single plain-English alert: what changed, when orders dropped, the likely culprit and the evidence for it, the estimated revenue lost, and how to fix it.

    You stay in control. Nothing is rolled back without your OK.

Features

Built to answer one question fast: what broke checkout?

  • AI incident analyst

    When checkout breaks, the analyst reads the stack trace and error message, the changelog and the code diff between the old and new plugin versions (public on WordPress.org), and your store's change timeline. Then it explains why checkout broke and how to fix it.

    It always shows a likely cause together with the evidence behind it, never a guess presented as fact.

    What it reads (example)

    • ErrorTypeError on /checkout/ in tracking.js
    • Changelog2.0.3: "Load checkout script earlier"
    • Diff2.0.2 → 2.0.3, checkout script enqueue moved
    • TimelineUpdated 03:04, orders stopped 03:10

  • Change timeline

    Every plugin, theme and core update, activation, and key settings change on your store, in one timeline.

  • Silent checkout breakage detection

    Catches JavaScript errors on checkout and membership signup pages, payment scripts that fail to load (Stripe, PayPal, Square, Braintree), payment error pages and gateway failures, not just crashes and downtime.

  • Slowness and overload alerts

    Times your site every five minutes and alerts you when it gets far slower than its usual speed, the warning sign of peak-hour overload before customers give up or the site goes down.

  • Likely culprit

    Lines up your changes against checkout health and points to the change most likely to blame, with a confidence level.

  • Revenue impact in your currency

    Compares orders with your normal pace for that time of day and week, so you see an estimate of what the breakage is costing.

  • Owner-confirmed undo

    Each alert shows how to undo the change. A rollback only happens when you confirm it.

  • Weekly "changes and their cost" email

    A short weekly summary of what changed on your store and what, if anything, it cost you.

  • Email and Slack alerts

    Alerts arrive where you already look, by email or in a Slack channel.

  • Built by a WooCommerce engineer

    Designed by someone who works on payment gateway and checkout code, and has traced these failures by hand.

FAQ

Questions site owners ask

What does the AI actually do?

It explains incidents. When checkout breaks, it reads the error messages, the changelog and code diff of whatever changed, and your store's timeline, then writes a plain-English explanation of the likely cause and suggests a fix. It does not take actions on its own. Every suggestion shows the evidence it is based on, and you approve anything that changes your store.

Does Culpritly slow my site down?

It is built not to. The connector plugin is lightweight and sends small batches of events in the background. It adds no front-end scripts to your site except a tiny error listener on checkout and other payment pages, so Culpritly can see the JavaScript errors your customers run into.

What data does Culpritly collect?

Change events (what was updated or changed, and when), error messages from checkout and other payment pages, and order counts and totals so Culpritly can learn your store's normal pace. The connector does not read card fields and is not built to collect card data, and no customer personal data beyond what is needed to do the job. To write AI explanations, error messages, change events and relevant public plugin code are sent to an AI provider for analysis. The privacy page has the details.

Will Culpritly roll things back automatically?

No. Every alert explains how to undo the change, and a rollback only happens when you confirm it. Rollback is never offered for WooCommerce core or for updates that changed your database, because reversing those safely needs a person's judgment. For those, the alert explains how to recover.

Which payment gateways does Culpritly support?

Culpritly is starting with Stripe, WooPayments and PayPal. It also notices when the Stripe, PayPal, Braintree or Square payment script fails to load on a payment page. More gateways are planned.

Does it only work with WooCommerce?

No. Culpritly watches WooCommerce checkout and cart, the payment pages of Restrict Content Pro, MemberPress and Paid Memberships Pro, and any page you mark as a payment page in the plugin settings. Change tracking, error alerts and slowness alerts work on any WordPress site. Order revenue estimates currently need WooCommerce.

Does Culpritly work with Shopify?

Not yet. Culpritly is for WordPress sites for now: WooCommerce stores and membership sites. Shopify support is planned for later.

How is this different from an activity log or an uptime monitor?

An activity log records changes. An uptime monitor checks whether your site is up. Neither tells you that checkout quietly stopped working, which change caused it, or why. Culpritly connects your store's changes to checkout breakage and to the revenue it is costing you, and its AI incident analyst explains the cause in plain English with the evidence. The upcoming update risk check goes one step earlier and flags risky updates before you install them.

Is Culpritly ready today?

Culpritly is in early access. Founding stores get the free breakage replay first, then the product as each part ships, at a price that stays locked while they are subscribed.
